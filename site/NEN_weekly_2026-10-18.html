遗传综合征/MEN1

MEN1 相关转移性 dpNET：PRRT 疗效与散发性相当；截断性基因型决定术后再干预与转移风险

Endocrine-Related Cancer 回顾性队列（19 例 MEN1 相关 dpNET vs 57 例散发性胰腺 NET）：177Lu-DOTATATE 后中位 PFS 39.6 vs 45.0 个月（HR 1.086）、OS 97.8 vs 89.3 个月，客观缓解率 44.4% vs 35.2%，≥3 级毒性无差异——MEN1 患者应按散发性相同指征使用 PRRT。同期 Surgery 单中心 153 例 MEN1-dpNET 手术队列（中位随访 16.4 年）：27% 需再次干预，截断性 MEN1 变异独立预测再干预（HR 3.82）与远处转移（HR 5.55），且发生转移者中 37.5% 最大病灶 ≤2 cm——基因型应与大小标准联合用于监测强度分层。

药物/系统治疗

Everolimus 起始剂量：标准剂量（10 mg）的表观生存优势经校正后消失，减量起始值得前瞻验证

Mayo Clinic/Cleveland 双中心回顾性队列（170 例晚期 NET）：10 mg 起始组 OS 81.5 vs 43.7 个月、rwPFS 12.4 vs 7.0 个月，但多变量校正后差异不再显著（OS HR 0.59, p=0.11；rwPFS HR 0.95, p=0.84）；10 mg 起始者 46.7% 需减量、任意级别毒性更高（89% vs 75%）。作者明确提示统计效力有限、不能证明剂量等效——回顾性「剂量-生存」证据的混杂结构（更差病例被迫减量）值得所有类似研究警惕。

影像/诊疗一体化

分子影像扩展：GLP-1R PET 定位 SSTR 阴性胃泌素瘤；双 SST2/SST5 拮抗性放射配体；CCK2R/GRPR 诊疗一体化

68Ga-exendin-4 PET/CT 在 3 例 DOTANOC 无摄取/不均匀摄取的转移性胃泌素瘤中灵敏检出原发灶与淋巴结、肝、骨转移，直接改变治疗决策（EJNMMI Reports）。RSC Medicinal Chemistry 以 DOTA-NOC 为骨架设计出 SST5 选择性拮抗配体 177Lu-10：SST2 摄取相当而 SST5 阳性肿瘤摄取高近 5 倍。Biomedicine & Pharmacotherapy 综述系统梳理 CCK2R/GRPR 的标记策略（68Ga/64Cu 诊断、177Lu/225Ac/212Pb 治疗）在 MTC、SCLC 等瘤种中的诊疗一体化前景——SSTR 之外的受体轴正在成为 NEN 分子影像的下一批靶点。

病理/分级分型

小 PanNET（≤2 cm）的 ALT 风险分层：CISH 平台可及，5 年 PFS 差异近 19 个百分点

多机构 172 例 ≤2 cm 小 PanNET 验证研究（Virchows Archiv）：ALT 阳性率 15.8%，阳性者 5 年 PFS 70.2% vs 88.8%（HR 4.69, p=0.002）；替代平台 ALT CISH 与 FISH 一致性 95.5%（阳性）/91.7%（阴性），普通明场显微镜即可判读——「观察 vs 手术」决策的可用标志物候选，正式分析验证与未选择队列确认是进入临床前的最后两步。

To evaluate the prognostic significance of locally advanced disease (LAD) in patients with pheochromocytomas and paragangliomas (PPGLs). We retrospectively analyzed 389 patients with histopathologically confirmed PPGLs from 19 tertiary centers. LAD was defined as capsular, periadrenal fat, adjacent-organ, or vascular invasion. 英文摘要原文

Primary hepatic neuroendocrine neoplasms (PHNENs) are exceedingly rare. Published evidence often lacks adequate clinicopathological support for a primary hepatic origin and uses inconsistent historical terminology. Previous reviews have pooled well-differentiated neuroendocrine tumours (NETs), poorly differentiated neuroendocrine carcinomas (NECs), and mixed neuroendocrine-non-neuroendocrine neoplasms (MiNENs), despite differences in biology, prognosis, and management. We systematically reviewed reported PHNENs using PRISMA-based screening and expert pathology reappraisal within the WHO 6th edition morphology-first framework. A systematic literature search was conducted in PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus, and Web of Science. Titles, abstracts, and full texts were screened according to predefined eligibility criteria; eligible articles underwent second-level expert assessment of pathology, including morphology (including NET G3 vs. NEC and MiNEN vs. amphicrine carcinoma), immunohistochemistry (expression of at least two NE markers), proliferation (Ki-67/mitotic activity), as well as clinical/imaging work-up to exclude metastases from extrahepatic primaries. Of the 67 potentially eligible studies submitted to expert pathology-based reappraisal, 28 studies (49.1%) were retained in the final dataset with 57 likely NEN cases (mainly case reports/small case series). Among the 57 cases, most were well-differentiated NETs (78.9%); NECs (8.8%), MiNENs (3.5%), and amphicrine carcinomas (1.8%) were less frequent. Some cases were high-grade NENs that could not be distinguished as well or poorly differentiated (7%). PHNEN literature is constrained by rarity, uncertainty of hepatic origin, and historical misclassification. Future studies and registries should report a minimum diagnostic dataset with standardised terminology according to the WHO 6th edition framework and in-depth clinical/imaging studies.

Merkel cell carcinoma (MCC) is a rare, aggressive neuroendocrine skin cancer. Treatment with avelumab (Programmed Death-Ligand 1 (PD-L1) antibody) is established in advanced disease, but real-world data are limited. Merkel cell carcinoma (MCC) represents a rare but highly aggressive neuroendocrine skin cancer with limited systemic therapy options for patients who fail or are ineligible for immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs). Intratumoral (IT) immunotherapy has emerged as a promising strategy, leveraging the accessibility of tumor lesions and their inherent capacity to trigger immune responses, known as abscopal effects. We outline MCC-specific rationale for routine lesion-level tracking, and recommendations for IT trial designs and objectives. Rigorous assessments of abscopal data will clarify IT's role as both rescue therapy and an ICI partner, potentially transforming outcomes in this refractory MCC.

Background/Objectives: Merkel cell carcinoma (MCC) is an aggressive neuroendocrine tumor, most commonly occurring on the head and neck. Incidence of MCC is rising with the aging population. Five-year overall survival (OS) for localized MCC is 50.6%; 5-year OS for patients with distant metastases is 13.5%. The purpose of this study is to determine if coordination of care for the treatment of MCC is logistically feasible, and provide a description of the patient population observed at this institution. Methods: All patients with MCC diagnosis from January 2012-June 2024 were identified at our tertiary care center. All patients with MCC were added to our non-melanoma skin cancer registry. Conclusions: MCC is a rare and aggressive tumor, often exhibiting subclinical tumor spread. Mohs micrographic surgery ensures 100% peripheral and deep margin assessment, with potential for tissue sparing. With thoughtful coordination, lymphoscintigraphy can be completed on the same day as Mohs surgery followed by sentinel lymph node biopsy (SLNB) and reconstructive surgery.
