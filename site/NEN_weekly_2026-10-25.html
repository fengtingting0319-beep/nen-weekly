Tarlatamab, a delta-like protein 3 (DLL3) × CD3-directed T cell engager (TCE), provides clinical efficacy against small cell lung cancers (SCLCs), although its efficacy remains limited, indicating the importance of clarifying the resistance mechanisms. We show that serotonin (5-HT) produced by DLL3high neuroendocrine cancers (NECs) induces immunosuppressive tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs), resulting in impaired CD8+ T cell function and TCE resistance. Streptozotocin (STZ)-based chemotherapy remains a treatment option for advanced pancreatic neuroendocrine tumours (PanNETs), particularly when tumour shrinkage is a therapeutic priority. However, its activity has been reported across heterogeneous treatment schedules, patient populations, and response-assessment eras. We conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis to estimate objective response rate (ORR) and landmark progression-free survival (PFS). We systematically reviewed randomised trials, prospective studies, and retrospective cohorts reporting ORR and/or PFS with STZ-containing regimens. Random-effects meta-analyses were conducted in a primary ELIGIBLE dataset restricted to advanced or metastatic pancreatic-only cohorts with standardised outcome assessment, and in a broader ALL dataset for contextual analyses. These single-arm pooled estimates do not establish superiority among STZ schedules or relative to other treatments, and the PFS benchmarks require cautious interpretation because of substantial clinical and methodological heterogeneity.

Peptide receptor radionuclide therapy (PRRT) is an established treatment of advanced well-differentiated neuroendocrine tumors (NETs) expressing somatostatin receptors. Although generally well tolerated, PRRT may precipitate or exacerbate pre-existing hormonal syndromes in patients with functioning NETs, leading to acute endocrine decompensation requiring urgent management. Current recommendations from the most important NET societies for prevention and treatment remain inconsistent, and available evidence is still fragmented. To address this gap, we conducted a comprehensive systematic review of the literature in PubMed, Scopus, and Embase up to May 2025, with the aim of identifying, organizing, and critically appraising the existing evidence on prevention and therapeutic strategies for managing hormonal crises in patients undergoing PRRT. Twenty-eight studies were included, comprising 18 case reports/series, 7 retrospective studies, and 3 prospective studies. Reported events included carcinoid crisis, insulinoma-related hypoglycemia, VIPoma-related secretory crisis, ectopic Cushing syndrome exacerbation, and catecholaminergic crisis in pheochromocytoma/paraganglioma. Carcinoid crisis was the most frequently reported endocrine emergency, with available cohort studies suggesting an incidence ∼1% during PRRT, although definitions and reporting remain heterogeneous. Hypoglycemic crises occurred mainly during the first PRRT cycle in patients with insulinoma, supporting the role of inpatient monitoring and preventive glucose-stabilizing strategies. Evidence for rarer syndromes was limited to small series and case reports but consistently emphasized pretreatment endocrine optimization, syndrome-specific prophylaxis, early recognition, and multidisciplinary management. Overall, available data remain insufficient to support firm recommendations but suggest that structured risk assessment, periprocedural monitoring, and harmonized management algorithms are essential to improve PRRT safety in functioning NETs.

英文摘要原文

Pulmonary carcinoids generally have favorable outcomes after surgery, but postoperative recurrence differs substantially by histological subtype. Existing guidance recognizes atypical histology and nodal involvement as adverse features, yet a focused quantitative synthesis of the magnitude and consistency of this association remains limited. MEDLINE/PubMed, Scopus, and Embase were searched from inception to July 12, 2026 for surgical cohorts reporting postoperative recurrence. Two reviewers independently screened titles and abstracts and assessed full-text eligibility, with disagreements resolved by consensus. The primary meta-analysis emphasized the risk ratio (RR) of observed recurrence in atypical versus typical carcinoids using random-effects models with restricted maximum likelihood and modified Hartung-Knapp intervals; odds ratios (ORs) and risk differences were complementary measures. Time-defined recurrence, adjusted prognostic factors, and anatomical patterns were synthesized separately because of incompatible outcome definitions and effect measures. Eighteen reports were included in the review, 11 contributed structured quantitative evidence, and 7 independent comparative cohorts entered the primary meta-analysis. Among 1074 resected tumors, recurrence occurred in 24 of 816 typical carcinoids and 51 of 258 atypical carcinoids. The pooled RR of observed recurrence was 6.57 (95% CI, 3.65-11.84), with a complementary OR of 8.08 (95% CI, 4.20-15.53). No statistical heterogeneity was detected (I2 = 0%), but this result was interpreted cautiously because only seven studies were available and clinically important differences in follow-up, staging, pathological review, and surveillance remained. Leave-one-out RR estimates ranged from 5.77 to 7.48. Time-defined evidence suggested recurrence of approximately 18%-25% by 5 years and 29%-32% by 10 years in atypical tumors, whereas node-negative typical tumors generally remained below 5% at 10 years, although late events occurred. Nodal involvement, advanced pT category, and larger tumor size were adverse prognostic features. Observed recurrence after resection was substantially more frequent in atypical than in typical pulmonary carcinoids. Histology and nodal status provide a clinically useful basis for postoperative risk stratification, but the available evidence does not establish the optimal surveillance modality, interval, or duration.

Somatostatin receptor (SSTR) PET has become a central imaging tool in neuroendocrine tumors (NETs), with established roles in detection, staging, restaging, and selection of patients for peptide receptor radionuclide therapy (PRRT). Despite its high sensitivity and target specificity, interpretation is not always straightforward. Physiological SSTR expression, focal variants of normal biodistribution, technical and administration-related artifacts, inflammatory conditions, and benign or malignant non-neuroendocrine lesions may all generate uptake that mimics NET involvement. Conversely, small lesion size, partial-volume effects, high physiological background, and genuinely low or absent receptor expression may reduce lesion conspicuity and lead to false-negative or underestimated disease. Interpretive challenges have also become more relevant as SSTR PET is increasingly used for quantitative, longitudinal, and theranostic assessment. Somatostatin analogue treatment can modify normal-organ biodistribution and lesion-to-background contrast, while liver and spleen uptake may vary substantially across serial examinations and should not necessarily be considered stable reference tissues. In addition, inter-lesional differences in receptor expression may have practical implications when SSTR PET is used to assess suitability for PRRT, because intense uptake in part of the disease does not necessarily imply adequate target expression across all clinically relevant lesions. This review summarizes the principal physiological variants and false-positive and false-negative findings encountered on SSTR PET and discusses contemporary pitfalls related to biodistribution, receptor heterogeneity, quantitative interpretation, and theranostic decision-making. Accurate interpretation requires integration of molecular, anatomical, and clinical information rather than reliance on isolated uptake intensity or a binary positive/negative classification.

英文摘要原文

This study clarifies the distinct clinicopathological and molecular features of amphicrine pancreatic carcinoma (AmpCA) through a case analysis and literature review. We present a case of AmpCA in a 73-year-old female, characterized by co-expression of acinar cell markers and neuroendocrine markers within individual tumor cells. Molecular profiling revealed high-risk mutations in KRAS G12V, TP53, and RB1, along with a high Ki-67 index (85%). Literature comparison confirms that AmpCA exhibits a unique bidirectional, single-cell differentiation pattern, fundamentally distinguishing it from the bicellular composition of mixed neuroendocrine-non-neuroendocrine neoplasms (MiNEN). We conclude that AmpCA is a distinct malignant entity requiring specific diagnostic criteria, and its molecular profile suggests potential for targeted therapies.

英文摘要原文

To report a case child of Pacak-Zhuang syndrome with EPAS1 p.Pro531Leu somatic variant in a child and to summarize the clinical characteristics, diagnosis, treatment methods, and prognosis of this syndrome based on a literature review. A retrospective analysis was conducted on the clinical data of a child girl with Pacak-Zhuang syndrome admitted to Hebei Children's Hospital in July 2019. Using the search terms 'Pacak-Zhuang syndrome', 'EPAS1', 'HIF-2α', 'HIF-2A', and 'Polycythemia-paraganglioma syndrome', relevant literature was retrieved from PubMed and Web of Science. The clinical manifestations, diagnostic methods, treatment plans, and outcomes of patients with this syndrome were analyzed. A 4-year-old girl had recurrent hypertension, polycythemia and renal hilar masses. Diagnosis was confirmed by imaging, biochemical detection and paraffin tissue genetic testing. She received three operations for paragangliomas; pathology showed CgA(+), Syn(+), NSE(+) and Ki‑67 ≤5%. Follow-up revealed tumor recurrence, but the condition improved with oral temozolomide. Combined with 10 retrieved articles, 18 patients (4 males,14 females, mean age 25 years) were analyzed. Polycythemia was the cardinal manifestation. All patients received surgery, and 10 suffered uncontrolled tumor recurrence. Pacak-Zhuang syndrome is a rare disorder involving the neuroendocrine and hematopoietic systems, often initially presenting with 'polycythemia'. Most cases are sporadic, with no apparent familial inheritance or history of other neuroendocrine tumors. When encountering pediatric patients with unexplained hypertension accompanied by polycythemia symptoms, the possibility of this syndrome should be considered, and further imaging studies and genetic testing are required for definitive diagnosis.
