Background/Objectives: Procalcitonin has been proposed as an alternative or complementary biomarker to calcitonin in medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC). We synthesised the published systematic reviews, appraised their quality, and measured their reliance on the same primary studies. Methods: Umbrella review of systematic reviews reporting measured procalcitonin values in proven or suspected MTC. PubMed/MEDLINE and the Cochrane Library were searched to 30 June 2026. Quality was appraised with AMSTAR-2 and overlap as the corrected covered area (CCA). Results: Seven evidence syntheses (2015-2024) were included, four addressing procalcitonin itself and two with meta-analysis. Neuroendocrine neoplasms (NENs) can be divided into well-differentiated neuroendocrine tumors and poorly differentiated carcinomas (NETs and NECs, respectively). Although the incidence of NENs is increasing globally, reports on palliative care in affected populations are scarce. This study aimed to describe the state of palliative care team (PCT) interventions for patients with NENs referred to tertiary care centers' PCTs. Among the patients with neoplasms referred to tertiary care centers' PCTs during January 2017-June 2025, those histopathologically diagnosed with NENs were included. They were classified as having NETs or NECs, and baseline characteristics, reason for PCT referral, and outcomes after PCT intervention were investigated. Additionally, for patients referred to a PCT because of pain, the numerical rating scale (NRS) score, cause of pain, pain type, pain management details, and the equivalent oral morphine dose (EOMD) were also investigated. Non-neoplastic pain was also identified in 5 patients (12.8%), for whom PCT-led opioid tapering and discontinuation were promoted to ensure appropriate opioid use. Given the diverse clinical course of NENs, research on palliative care and pain control for affected patients must be further deepened.

英文摘要原文

Although the International Medullary Thyroid Carcinoma Grading System (IMTCGS) was proposed to standardize the histologic grading of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC), the validity of its 5% Ki-67 index cutoff and the optimal assessment method remain to be fully established. This multicenter study aimed to validate the Ki-67 index component of the IMTCGS in a Korean cohort using digital image analysis (DIA) and to determine the most clinically relevant evaluation method. We retrospectively analyzed 187 surgically resected MTC cases across 16 institutions. Ki-67 indices were evaluated via DIA using two approaches: the hotspot method (highest proliferative area) and the average method (across three representative regions). Prognostic values of various Ki-67 cutoffs (5%, 3%, and ROC curve-derived thresholds) were evaluated independently and within the IMTCGS framework. IMTCGS high-grade status incorporating the 5% hotspot Ki-67 index emerged as an independent prognostic factor for recurrence-free survival (RFS), along with male sex, elevated postoperative serum calcitonin levels, and pN1b status. IMTCGS grading using the 5% average Ki-67 index failed to retain independent significance. While the composite high-grade IMTCGS classification remained an independent predictor of RFS in multivariate analysis, none of its constituent components (tumor necrosis, mitotic count, or Ki-67 index) retained independent prognostic significance when evaluated separately. This multicenter study validates the prognostic utility of the IMTCGS in Korean MTC patients. DIA-based hotspot Ki-67 assessment provides superior prognostic stratification compared to the average method, supporting its preference for routine clinical IMTCGS implementation.

Management of Zollinger-Ellison syndrome (ZES) in patients with multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1 (MEN1) remains controversial, particularly in non-metastatic disease where the role of surgery is debated. In contrast, treatment of metastatic disease is guided by established systemic approaches. We aimed to evaluate stage-dependent clinical decision-making in MEN1-related ZES using standardized clinical vignettes across ENETS Centers of Excellence (CoEs). A questionnaire-based study using two clinical vignettes was conducted by the ENETS MEN1 Task Force across all 68 ENETS CoEs, with a predefined participation target of 70%. Additional non-ENETS expert centers were also invited. Dedicated multidisciplinary teams provided institutional consensus responses. Scenarios included non-metastatic disease, locally metastatic disease, and distant metastatic disease. Centers were stratified by volume using a cutoff of 30 MEN1 patients in active care. Data were analyzed descriptively, comparing management strategies between low- and high-volume centers. Fifty-seven centers participated (52 of 68 ENETS CoEs [76.5%] and 5 additional non-ENETS expert centers). All 57 centers completed the questionnaire in full. All cases received symptomatic therapy with proton pump inhibitors. In non-metastatic disease, management was heterogeneous, with 56.1% recommending surgery. Centre volume strongly influenced decisions (73.1% vs. 41.9% surgery in low- vs. high-volume centers, p = .018). Preferred surgical approaches included duodenotomy with regional lymphadenectomy (43.9%) and the Thompson procedure (left pancreatectomy, duodenal exploration with excision of tumors and node dissection, enucleation of pancreatic head lesions; 26.3%). In early disease, decision-making-particularly regarding surgical versus medical management-is highly variable and influenced by center experience, whereas advanced disease shows more consistent, guideline-aligned treatment patterns. The high participation rate across ENETS CoEs suggests that these findings are likely to be relevant to expert neuroendocrine tumor practice.

英文摘要原文

Medullary thyroid cancer (MTC) is a rare but aggressive neuroendocrine malignancy arising from parafollicular C cells. Despite its low incidence, MTC has historically been estimated to account for 13.4% of thyroid cancer-related deaths. Survival outcomes remain limited in advanced disease, with a historical cohort reporting a 10-year survival of 21% for stage IV MTC, supporting the need for novel therapeutic approaches. Carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA), a fetal glycoprotein minimally expressed in normal adult tissues, is elevated in various malignancies and plays an established role in carcinogenesis, metastasis, and therapy resistance. In MTC, CEA is produced by neoplastic C cells alongside calcitonin, and both serum and tumor-associated CEA levels are markedly elevated relative to healthy tissue. More than half of MTC patients demonstrate elevated CEA, and intense CEA immunohistochemical staining correlates with aggressive, disseminated disease, highlighting its potential utility as both a biomarker and investigational therapeutic target in the disease. For MTC, surgery is the mainstay for locoregional disease with the intent for complete surgical resection when possible. Systemic therapy is generally reserved for structurally progressive, unresectable, symptomatic, or threatening advanced disease, with selective RET inhibitors used in RET-mutant disease and multi-kinase TKIs as additional treatment options. Although TKIs have improved progression-free survival, treatment can be limited by toxicities and the development of resistance over time. These limitations underscore the need for novel therapeutic strategies that can achieve more durable disease control and improve overall survival. Given its strong association with tumor burden, disease progression, and aggressive pathology, CEA represents a promising investigational target for therapeutic exploration in MTC. A narrative literature review was performed to evaluate carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) as a therapeutic target and biomarker in medullary thyroid cancer (MTC). Searches were conducted in 2025 and 2026 using PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Cochrane Library, Scopus, Web of Science, and ClinicalTrials.gov. The current literature supports further investigation of CEA targeting as a therapeutic strategy in MTC. However, CEA is not yet a clinically validated therapeutic target and clinical trial data remain limited.

Pancreatic neuroendocrine neoplasms (PNENs) represent a rare, heterogeneous group of tumors with highly variable clinical outcomes. While standard clinicopathological parameters like World Health Organization (WHO) grading and TNM staging provide critical prognostic data, they do not fully explain the survival disparities observed in patients with similar tumor characteristics.
